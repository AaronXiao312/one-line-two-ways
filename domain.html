DOMAIN KNOWLEDGE / SYSTEM DESIGN

Read the route,
not just the number.

The game is based on a real navigation mistake: two services with the same line number can follow different branches.

THE DOMAIN

What a rider needs to notice

A metro line number tells you which service family you are using. It may not tell you which branch or terminal the arriving train serves. Riders need to compare their destination with the train's terminal, platform direction, route map and next-stop display.

For this prototype, the station names and branch layout are fictionalized. The route-reading decision is inspired by the designer's Shanghai Line 10 experience; the map is not a real-world navigation guide.

THE NOVICE MISCONCEPTION

“Same number means same route.”

A beginner may board the first train with the expected line number and only discover the branch mismatch after the train departs. An experienced rider checks the terminal and compares the path with the destination first.

THE LEARNING SHIFT

From number-matching to route-checking.

Players practice reading branch cues before boarding, then checking the next stop while in motion. If they make a wrong choice, the route display shows where the train diverges and gives them a chance to transfer or turn back.

SYSTEM GRAPH

Observe → judge → act → adjust.

ENVIRONMENT

Destination, branch map, terminals, train arrival, service alert, transfer point and deadline.

PLAYER ACTIONS

Inspect signs, compare routes, wait, board, exit, transfer and return.

CALCULATED RESULTS

Route match, waiting time, detour minutes, transfers and arrival status.

FEEDBACK

Highlighted path, terminal and next-stop signs, updated ETA, route alert and outcome message.
